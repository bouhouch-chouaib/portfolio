## The problem

Zéro Wattheure supports professionals through their energy transition and regulatory compliance — the French Décret Tertiaire, Décret BACS and the CSRD Directive. The company is based in the EDF Lab ecosystem in Palaiseau.

The first commercial contact on its website goes through a conversational assistant. Technically, it worked: the connection to the Gemini API was operational. **Commercially, it was failing.**

Two precise defects. First, it explained to visitors for free how to use the French government's OPERAT platform — that is, it gave away the service the company sells. Second, it qualified nobody: it answered any visitor indifferently, without trying to find out whether they were a prospect, nor collecting their contact details.

The result was visible in the weekly indicators: conversations going nowhere and almost no contact requests. The tool was costing money without fulfilling its function.

## The constraints

- **No third-party SDK.** The company mandated direct HTTP calls to Google's REST API, without going through an SDK.
- **A team that would take over the code after I left.** Any solution requiring heavy maintenance was disqualified in advance, however sophisticated.
- **A modest but living document corpus**: about ten institutional web pages and a few commercial PDFs, expected to change.
- **No tolerable latency for the visitor.** A user who waits leaves the site.

## What I measured

- **Cache efficiency**: around 6,200 tokens processed per request, of which more than 6,190 came from the cache — over 99 %. The real compute cost only covers the few dozen tokens of the question asked.
- **Robustness testing**: 10 tests out of 10 passed, across four categories. Basic safety (refusing out-of-scope subjects: tax advice, programming, physical emergencies). Commercial hallucination (no invented prices, features or laws). Commercial behaviour (resisting an attempt to hijack the role, a disguised OPERAT support request, a non-professional visitor). A complete qualification path on a case involving several buildings and several regulatory thresholds.
- **Corpus ingested**: 11 sites scraped in parallel, plus the commercial PDFs provided by the company.
- **Perceived latency**: none for the visitor, since document extraction and caching happen entirely upstream, before the first question.

One nuance I want to keep: the saving on requests comes with a cost of storing and maintaining the cache at the provider. The gain is real; it is not free.

## What went wrong

Two blockers, and they tell the same story.

The first was the structure of the API responses. Gemini does not return a sentence, it returns a nested JSON object: a dictionary containing a list, containing a dictionary, containing a list. I lost a considerable amount of time extracting the only thing I cared about — the response text — before understanding that I had to read that structure as a data schema, rather than groping through it.

The second was tuning the model's behaviour. Gemini's documentation describes what each parameter does, not what it produces on a real case. I spent several days varying the settings and the prompt while observing the effects, with no manual telling me what to choose.

These two difficulties marked the passage between two things I had been confusing: **making an API call work, and understanding what I am manipulating.** The first takes an hour. The second took two weeks, and it is what allowed me to write a system prompt that holds and to pass ten tests designed to break it.

## What I take away

Three things.

The maintenance cost of a solution matters as much as its performance. I could have delivered a RAG and presented it as more accomplished; it would have been abandoned three months after I left.

A language model without an explicit frame spontaneously adopts the most helpful behaviour possible — which, in a commercial context, amounts to giving away for free what the company charges for. **The behaviour of an AI system is designed; it is not obtained by default.**

Finally, evaluating a non-deterministic system requires building tests that look for failure rather than success. All ten scenarios I wrote were aimed at derailing the assistant.
