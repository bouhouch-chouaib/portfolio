## The problem

Organisations running on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 struggle to know where they stand: the actual configuration of their environment changes constantly, and checking it against their compliance requirements is manual work few teams have time for.

Aleth connects to these environments and turns them into actionable **indicators** and PDF reports. The positioning is deliberate: the product provides indicators to support decisions — never verdicts. <Todo>specify the target customer segment and the typical buying trigger</Todo>

## The constraints

- **Sensitive data that isn't ours.** The product reads information about its customers' work environment; every requested access scope must be defensible in front of a CISO.
- **A real company, with competitors.** This page covers the problem, standard architecture choices and deliverables — deliberately nothing about the product's internal mechanics.
- **A small team.** The product is built and maintained alongside my final year of studies: every technical choice has to save maintenance time.
- **Real billing.** A Stripe subscription flow in production, which means webhooks to make reliable and account states to handle properly.

## The decisions

<Decision
  n="01"
  statut="adopted"
  titre="Strictly read-only integrations"
  contexte="The product needs to connect to customer environments through the Google Workspace Admin SDK and Microsoft Graph."
  retenu="OAuth 2.0 with scopes limited to read access. The access surface is minimal, the security case fits in one sentence in front of a CISO, and no code error can ever modify a customer's environment."
  ecarte="Write scopes, which would have enabled automated remediation: the added value justified neither the risk nor the extra friction in the sales conversation."

<Decision
  n="02"
  statut="adopted"
  titre="A self-managed VPS rather than a PaaS"
  contexte="Hosting the FastAPI backend, the Next.js frontend and PDF report generation, on a young company's budget."
  retenu="A hand-administered Linux VPS with nginx in front: low fixed cost, full control over configuration and over where the data lives."
  ecarte="A managed PaaS: faster to set up, but costs grow with usage and there is less control over the exact infrastructure configuration."
